The event-site schedule says "10:30AM Submissions Due!", but Devpost's deadline and its requirements text both say 10:00 AM. Treat 10:00 AM as the real cutoff.

## Submission rules

- Exactly **one title track**.
- Up to **three sponsored side tracks**, plus any number of non-sponsored side tracks.
- Some side tracks judge from a **video submission** instead of an in-person round. The Hacker Guide says which ones.
- Teams of up to four. The project must be started and finished during DubHacks '26. The MLH code of conduct applies.
- Eligibility: 18+, college students.

## Judging criteria (all tracks)

| Criterion           | Weight | What it covers                                             |
| ------------------- | ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Functionality       | 30%    | Code functionality and quality of execution                |
| Track applicability | 30%    | How closely the project relates to its chosen track        |
| Design              | 20%    | User interface and aesthetics                              |
| Creativity          | 20%    | A unique or new solution to a problem, with few weaknesses |

Devpost lists one judge by name: **Lisa Kapil (Visa)**.

What this means for us: 60% is "does it work" plus "is it obviously this track". A reliable end-to-end demo that uses Visa's own words (discovery, decision-making) is worth more than extra features. Design is 20%, so dashboard polish still counts.

## Our targets

### Visa: Reimagine Shopping (title track): $5,000 cash, 1 winner

The prompt from the event site, paraphrased: use generative AI to build a smarter, more intuitive, more personalized commerce experience that transforms one or more stages of the shopping journey:

- discovery
- decision-making
- personalization
- checkout
- payments
- loyalty and rewards
- post-purchase

The event site shows Visa as the fourth track alongside the three themed title tracks, so we're treating it as our one title track. That means we can't also enter Community Kitchen, Chef's Choice, or Cook Smarter. Confirm this in the Hacker Guide.

Fit: our angle is **discovery** (and decision-making). AgentProof checks whether AI shopping agents can find and choose the right product on a real storefront, then repairs the store when they can't. Since track applicability is 30%, say "discovery" explicitly in the Devpost writeup and the pitch.
